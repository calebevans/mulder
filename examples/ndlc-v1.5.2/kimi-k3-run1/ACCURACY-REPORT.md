## Analysis of Misses and Errors

### What v1.5.2 changed

The #228 fix scopes `tsk.partitions` lookups to the image being analysed and scans every partition in `detect_masquerading`. On this evidence set the masquerade line counts are the same as the pre-release build (0 on rm1, 17 on rm2, 3 on rm3) and `optical.listing` is again 58 lines, so the extractor output the model consumed is unchanged. What is new in this run is the model's use of `extract_optical_file` (38 calls, none in run 4): it pulled the disc's files out and ran ExifTool and YARA over them, which is where finding 12's content characterisation, the Govdocs identification and the omb.eop.gov counter-analysis come from. The 4 `detect_masquerading` calls (run 4: 20) were enough for item 7.

### Drive letters inverted (FPs 3, 4; items 4, 5, 17)

The run read RecentDocs "BD-RE Drive (D:) IAMAN CD", wrote "D: is a BD-RE (Blu-ray) drive labeled 'IAMAN CD'! Interesting - so D: might be an optical drive, OR the rm2 device", noted "BD-RE is Blu-ray Rewritable. But rm2 is FAT32 1GB. Hmm.", and resolved it by label: "the volume label 'IAMAN $_@' (garbled) on rm2 matches 'IAMAN CD'. So rm2 (D:) is the 'IAMAN' device." From that decision follow the evening `D:\de` folder creation and browsing assigned to RM2 (they are the CD mastering, Q33/Q35), and, since `E:` then had to be RM1 all afternoon, the 13:59–14:01Z ShellBags on RM2 (Q26) assigned to RM1 as a "staging" copy with "real filenames" exemplified by a masqueraded one. Run 4 had `D:` consistently as the optical drive and asserted no dated copy onto RM1. The same decision truncated RM2's label to "IAMAN" (item 4).

### The five documents unnamed (items 6, 16)

RM1's `tsk.filelist` (27 lines) and `tsk.timeline` (67 lines) were indexed and never read for names or for February. The parallel agent that did source RM1 (`tsk.fsstat`, `tsk.filelist`, `tsk.partitions`) submitted a critical finding titled "USB Device with Secret Project Data Connected and Data Copied to Network Share", retitled it "USB Device 'Authorized USB' Connected with Secret Project Data", and the model deleted it as a duplicate at 20:34:39 without merging its content. Two of the five names (`design_concept.ppt`, `proposal.docx`) were read from ShellBags in-run and not delivered. Without the originals, the disc's 17 files could only be matched to RM2's copies and to the share's folder names, which keeps item 16 at PARTIAL despite the richest content analysis of the disc in the series.

### CCleaner and the RM2 deletion (FPs 1, 5; items 14, 15)

Run 4's CCleaner false positive was the generic one ("deliberate evidence destruction"). This run reverts to the baseline's specific form, attributing the unrecoverable browser history to CCleaner, and adds a second attribution: the RM2 orphans are read as the product of the Mar 25 wiping, although finding 2 dates their creation to Mar 24 and finding 4 dates the tools' installers to Mar 25. Neither the CCleaner uninstall nor Eraser's actual target (`\Desktop\temp`, Q52) is reported.

### Found in-run, not delivered (items 1, 2, 18, 19)

"Iaman Informant" with the resignation letter, `informant-PC` (16 log hits), `spy.conspirator@nist.gov` ("another interesting NIST-affiliated address") and the two files opened from the share were all read during the run and are absent from the report. None was in a deleted finding; they were never written into one. Items 1, 2 and 18 keep their run-4 status on thinner evidence; item 19 keeps the baseline's PARTIAL but loses run 4's named correspondent.

### Registry basics (items 12, 20) and search history (item 9)

`TimeZoneInformation` reached a finding for the first time in this series (item 20 FOUND), even though the narrative then mixes FAT-local and UTC times in Q6. `ProductName` was still never queried and `System.evtx` never indexed; the USBSTOR last-write times (both the key's values, including RM1's Mar 23 first connection) are delivered without device attribution. Search history recovers from run 4's regression: twenty-plus verbatim queries with hit counts, both the leakage and anti-forensics halves, from `bulk.url_searches` rather than page visits inferred from `bulk.url`.

### Twelve findings against thirty-one

Run 4 delivered 31 findings (38 in the case, 7 merged with descriptions preserved by `deduplicate_findings`); this run delivered 12 (23 in the case, 8 removed by `delete_finding`, 3 negatives shown separately). The count itself cost little: the narrative sections carry most of what run 4 spread across findings (accounts, network configuration, Chrome, the Drive install chain, the disc's sessions and decoys, the negatives), and the seven FOUND items are all fully evidenced inside the twelve. What the compaction did cost is the material that lived in the deleted or never-written findings and had no other home: RM1's real filenames and the Feb 15 window (items 6, 13), the resignation letter and hostname (items 1, 2), the correspondent and the opened share files (items 18, 19), `icloudsetup.exe`'s execution (item 11), the confirmed-confidence Drive finding (item 10 stays FOUND at inference). Five items fell against run 4, one rose to MISSED, and every one of them is a fact the run had seen. The three new false positives are unrelated to the count; they are reading errors in the findings that were kept.
